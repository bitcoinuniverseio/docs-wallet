# Backup

This page covers the one artifact that matters: your recovery phrase, how the wallet helps you verify it, and how to keep it safe for years.

## What the backup is

Your 12-word Secret Recovery Phrase encodes every account in that wallet, on every network and address type. One correct copy restores everything in this extension or in any compatible wallet. Your password is not a backup; it only unlocks this device.

## Rules that do not bend

- Write the words on paper or metal, in order. Offline means offline.
- Never photograph the phrase, store it in a file or password manager synced to the cloud, or read it aloud on a call.
- Never type it into a website. The only legitimate places are the extension's own restore flow and a wallet you chose to migrate to.
- No one legitimate asks for it. Not Universe, not support, not an airdrop.

## Verification, at creation and after

At creation the wallet runs a **Backup quiz**: you type three words from your written copy before the wallet is created, and it must read **Backup verified**. That proves the paper, not your memory.

The Security Dashboard tracks this state, showing **Recovery ready** or **Backup pending**, and its **Recovery** tab keeps the next action in front of you.

## Re-reading the phrase later

**Settings → Secret Recovery Phrase** reveals the phrase again, behind your password, with a deliberately slow, private flow: unlock only when alone, write in order, do not screenshot. Use it to re-verify a stored backup or to create a second copy. Treat every reveal as an event; if anyone might have seen the words, move funds to a fresh wallet.

## A backup drill worth doing

Once funds are meaningful, prove the exit: read your paper copy and check three scattered words against a fresh reveal. If you hold large amounts, restore the phrase into a second device or a compatible wallet with a trivial balance first, so the day you must recover is not the first time you tried. [Restore](restore.md) describes what to expect.

## Storage that survives years

- Two copies, two places, both under your control. A single copy is one flood away from loss.
- Metal outlasts paper if fire or water is plausible.
- Anyone who can read a copy owns your funds; store accordingly.
