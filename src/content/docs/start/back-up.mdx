**Intended reader:** anyone whose wallet holds, or will hold, anything they would mind losing.
**Goal:** a physical record of the phrase that you can find in five years.
**Prerequisites:** the wallet, your password, pen and paper, a private room.
**Safety:** this is the highest-risk routine action in the whole product. Everything else can be
retried.

## The rule

Your recovery phrase is the wallet. Not a hint, not a reset code. Whoever has the words has the coins.

## Where to write it

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| Pen on paper, or stamped metal | Photograph it |
| Two copies, in two separate places | Store it in cloud notes, email, or chat |
| Numbered, in order | Type it into any website, ever |
| Somewhere a house fire or a burglary would not reach both copies | Store it in a password manager you do not control the sync of |
| Somewhere someone you trust could find it if you could not | Split it across services and hope |

Order is part of the secret. Twelve correct words in the wrong order is not your wallet.

## Steps

1. Open **Settings**, then **Export recovery phrase**.
2. Enter your password.
3. Write the words on paper, numbered, in order.
4. Close the screen before you do anything else.
5. Put the paper where you decided it goes, now, not later.

## How to verify the backup works

A backup you have never tested is a hope, not a backup.

1. Note the first receiving address of your wallet.
2. In a separate browser profile, install the extension and restore from the paper copy alone. Do not
   look at the screen version.
3. Confirm the same first address appears.
4. Remove that test wallet from the second profile.

If the address matches, your paper copy is correct and complete. If it does not, fix it while you can.

## Expected result

You can reconstruct the wallet from paper alone, with no device, no password, and no help.

## Common failures

| What happens | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| The restored wallet shows a different address | Usually a different address type, not a wrong phrase. Compare all four. See [Address types](/docs-wallet/concepts/address-types). |
| A word is illegible on the paper | Rewrite the whole phrase from the wallet now, while you still can unlock it. |
| You used a BIP-39 passphrase and did not record it | The phrase alone is not enough. Record the passphrase separately, in a different place. |

## Recovery path

While you can still unlock the wallet, every backup problem is fixable: open the export screen and
write it again. Once you cannot unlock it and the paper is wrong, there is no path. That asymmetry is
the whole reason to do this today.
